Over the past months, I have been working on developing a new digital platform for my Manufacturing Marketing Agency. The idea behind the project was clear from the beginning: to create a website that would do more than simply present a list of marketing services. It needed to reflect the way manufacturing and industrial businesses operate, how their products and markets differ, and what potential customers need to understand before choosing a supplier or a business partner.

Manufacturing is a broad and technically diverse field. A company producing metal structures has different products, customers, and sales processes from a manufacturer of industrial equipment, electrical components, building materials, or specialized machinery. A marketing website for this sector should take these differences into account.

That is why I decided to approach the project as the development of a structured platform rather than a conventional agency website.

@@ Building the structure around the manufacturing industry

One of the first tasks was to establish the website's architecture.

The main sections were planned around three connected areas: Services, Industries, and Case Studies. Each has a specific purpose, but they are also designed to work together.

The Services section explains the marketing and digital work available to manufacturing companies. The Industries section organizes the website around different manufacturing sectors and their specific business contexts. Case Studies provide examples of previous projects and demonstrate the practical experience behind the work.

These sections are not intended to exist independently. Their connections help visitors move from an industry or business need to relevant services and examples of completed work.

The Industries section required particular attention. Manufacturing includes many different fields, and a single general category would not provide enough detail. I organized the structure around nine main industrial sectors, with more specific manufacturing directions within them.

This creates room to expand the website gradually. New areas can be developed as dedicated content becomes available, without having to redesign the entire structure each time.

@@ Making case studies useful

Case studies are an important part of a manufacturing marketing website. However, simply displaying a company name, a picture, and a short description does not tell a potential client very much.

I wanted each case study to provide a clearer picture of the business, its products, the market it operates in, and the work carried out on the project. The content structure also makes it possible to connect a case study with relevant industries and services.

This is particularly important in manufacturing, where experience in one field may be relevant to another. A project involving custom metal fabrication, for example, may demonstrate experience with industrial production, complex product catalogs, or B2B marketing processes.

The goal is to give visitors enough context to understand why a particular project is relevant, rather than presenting a collection of unrelated portfolio entries.

@@ Developing the platform behind the website

A significant part of the work took place behind the visible pages.

The website is built using Next.js and React, with Payload CMS managing its content and Vercel used for deployment. The content model was developed around the needs of the project, including custom fields, collections, and relationships between different types of content.

This approach makes it possible to manage services, industries, case studies, and articles as connected parts of one platform. It also provides a foundation for adding new content types and expanding the website over time.

During development, AI-based platforms were used as part of the working process. They supported different stages of research, planning, development, and review. The focus was not on using a particular tool, but on applying these technologies where they could make the work more effective.

The important part is the resulting architecture: a content system that can grow with the business and support more than a fixed set of pages.

@@ SEO and visibility in AI-powered search

Search visibility was considered from the beginning of the project, rather than treated as something to add after the website had been built.

The structure of the pages, the relationships between content, and the organization of services and industries all contribute to a clearer website architecture. The aim is to make the content understandable both to visitors and to search engines.

Traditional SEO remains important, particularly for manufacturing companies whose potential customers search for specific products, technologies, services, and production capabilities.

At the same time, the way people discover information is changing. AI-powered systems such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity are becoming another way to research companies, compare solutions, and find suppliers.

Visibility in these systems is not achieved simply by adding a few AI-related keywords to a website. It depends on the quality and clarity of the information, the depth of relevant expertise, and the credibility of a company's presence across the wider web.

For this reason, the next stage of work will include developing more detailed industry content, publishing useful professional materials, and strengthening the website's presence through relevant external sources and publications.

@@ Usability, design, and technical foundations

The visual direction of the website was developed around a clean, bright, and professional presentation. Manufacturing and industrial subjects can be technically complex, so the design needs to make information easy to navigate without distracting from the content.

The website structure also takes mobile use into account. Navigation, page layouts, and content presentation need to remain clear across different screen sizes.

Technical performance is another area that will be measured and refined as the platform moves into active use. The current development stage establishes the technical foundation; ongoing testing and optimization will be part of the next phase.

@@ Preparing for promotion and further development

With the main website structure in place, the project is ready to move toward its next stage: developing its public presence and attracting relevant manufacturing and industrial businesses.

This will include working with LinkedIn, Google Ads, Facebook, and other suitable channels. The website now provides a foundation for presenting services, demonstrating experience, and directing potential clients to information relevant to their industries and business needs.

Another important direction will be the development of a Resources section.

I plan to expand the website with practical Guides, White Papers, articles, research, and other professional materials. These resources will cover manufacturing markets, technologies, business challenges, and digital marketing approaches relevant to industrial companies.

I also want to develop free online tools that can provide practical value to visitors. These will be considered as part of the broader platform, rather than as isolated additions.

The content itself will grow alongside the website. More detailed coverage of individual industries, production processes, technologies, and specialized business areas will help make the platform a useful source of information, not just a presentation of agency services.

@@ The first stage is complete. The next stage begins.

This stage of development was about building the foundation: defining the structure, organizing the content, developing the CMS, connecting the main sections, and establishing the technical platform.

It is not the end of the project. A platform like this needs to develop continuously as new content, industry knowledge, tools, and practical experience are added.

But the essential foundation is now in place. The next step is to put it to work: expand the content, develop useful resources, strengthen search visibility, and begin active promotion.

The goal remains the same: to help manufacturing and industrial companies present their capabilities more effectively and build a stronger digital presence in the markets they serve.
