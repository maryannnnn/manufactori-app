Buyers still type queries into Google and Bing. Some of them also ask ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity to shortlist suppliers, explain a standard, or compare two methods. Those products do not share one ranking formula, and none of them owes a manufacturer a citation. What you can do is make the public site accurate, crawlable, and specific enough that a person or a retrieval system can tell what you manufacture.

This article separates on-site clarity, technical access, and off-site reputation. It complements the earlier note on what GEO means for a B2B company. It does not replace a conventional SEO programme, and it does not claim that any assistant will recommend the company.

@@ Traditional results and AI-assisted answers are not the same job

A classic results page is a list of documents. The reader opens the pages and compares them. An AI-assisted answer is a composed response. The system may retrieve pages, product data, or previously indexed text and then write a summary. Google’s AI features, Bing’s answers, and the chat products named above do this in different ways. Some lean on a live web index. Some lean on a model’s stored training material plus a search tool. Some show citations. Some do not.

Because the mechanisms differ, a tactic copied from a blog post about “ranking in ChatGPT” is not evidence. Google has published notes on AI features that still require an indexed, eligible page. OpenAI, Google, and Perplexity describe their products differently, and those descriptions change. Treat vendor documentation as the source for how a product works today. Treat marketing claims about secret ranking factors as speculation.

The practical overlap is smaller and more useful. If a page cannot be fetched, it cannot be quoted from the live web. If the page does not state what the product is, a summary has to guess. If the only public description is a slogan, the guess will be vague or wrong.

@@ Crawlability, entities, and technical evidence

Crawlability means a search engine can reach the URL, read the important text, and understand which pages you want indexed. That is ordinary technical SEO: stable URLs, internal links, a sitemap of real pages, and text in HTML rather than only inside an image or a locked file. An assistant that browses the web depends on the same access. An assistant that does not browse cannot be steered by a sitemap at all.

An entity, in this context, is a thing you can name consistently: the company, a product family, an application, a material, a standard, or a plant capability. Pages should state the relationships in sentences a specialist would accept. “We machine 316 stainless valve bodies for washdown lines up to the pressure on the datasheet” is an entity statement. “Innovative solutions for industry” is not.

Technical evidence is the part a buyer can check: a dimension with a unit, a standard you build to, a limit with its test condition, a drawing, or a case description that does not invent results. Our article on first-party technical evidence covers how to publish those facts without pretending they are a ranking boost. Original evidence helps a reader decide. It is not a lever that forces an answer engine to cite you.

@@ Make products, applications, and expertise readable

Structure the site so each important fact has a home. The company page says what you manufacture and where you operate, without a fake office list. Product and category pages carry specifications and exclusions. Application pages describe the job and the products that fit it. Service or capability pages describe the work you sell, such as making that information easier to retrieve, only when that is actually your offer. For a manufacturer, the equivalent is the process or the product range.

Use headings that name the contents. Put the critical numbers in the HTML, not only in a PDF. Repeat the product name in a way that stays natural, and use the same name on the datasheet, the page title, and the quote form. Inconsistent names split one product into several vague ones.

Answer the questions sales already receives, in full sentences, on the page where the question belongs. A short answer under a descriptive heading is easier to verify than the same fact buried in a paragraph about the company’s history. This is good editing. It is not a schema trick, and it does not require a special “AI page”.

@@ What belongs on the site, and what belongs off it

On-site work is the part you control: information architecture, wording, internal links, indexation, and the accuracy of specifications. Technical accessibility is the part that decides whether that information can be fetched and rendered. Content structure is how a reader moves from a product to an application to a limit. These three can all be reviewed on your own domain.

Off-site authority is the public record you do not fully control. Relevant publications, supplier directories that are actually used in your industry, standards bodies, and editorial links can confirm that the company exists and does the work the site describes. A mention helps when it is specific and when it matches the site. A block of identical descriptions posted on low-quality directories does not become authority because someone called it a citation strategy.

Keep the two conversations separate in planning. A broken canonical tag is not fixed by a guest article. A guest article is not a substitute for a product page that states the operating limit. Our note on mentions and entity consistency is about the public record. The website still has to agree with that record.

@@ How to watch visibility without inventing a score

There is no stable public score for “AI visibility” that you should manage as a KPI. You can still watch a few observable things:

  • Whether Google Search Console shows the important catalogue URLs as indexed, and which queries already bring impressions.
  • Whether Bing Webmaster Tools shows the same core URLs as known, if Bing matters to your buyers.
  • A small, repeated set of buyer questions that you ask in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity, recorded with the date and the wording. Note whether your company, your product names, or your competitors appear, and whether the answer is accurate.
  • Referral visits or assisted enquiries that sales can tie to a page, when the analytics you already run can see them.

Do this as a qualitative review, not as a league table. The same question can produce different answers on different days, in different countries, and on paid versus free tiers. A missing mention is not a penalty. A mention that describes the wrong product is a correction job on your own pages and, where you can, on the third-party page that is out of date.

Write down what you will not claim. Do not tell the board that the company will be recommended, cited, or included in an overview. Report what the site now states, which URLs are indexed, and what a sample of answers got right or wrong.

@@ Where this work sits next to ordinary SEO

AI search optimisation does not retire title tags, internal links, or catalogue hygiene. Those decide whether the useful pages exist in the index that several of these systems still consult. The additional demand is editorial: say what the product is, what it is not, and which evidence supports the claim.

If the budget is limited, fix retrieval of the core catalogue first. Then tighten the sentences that define products and applications. Then look at external mentions that are wrong or missing in places your buyers already read. A programme that starts with prompts and ignores the product pages will have nothing solid to be summarised.

The sensible outcome is modest. Buyers who use a conventional results page and buyers who use an assistant both encounter a description your engineers recognise. Some answers will cite you. Many will not. The site’s job is to remain the accurate source either way.
