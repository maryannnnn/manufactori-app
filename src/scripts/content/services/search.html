Engineers and purchasing teams still type specific queries: a standard, a material, a machine type, a replacement part. Search work makes the pages that answer those queries crawlable and accurate. AI assistants are a second reading of the same facts, not a separate promise of citations or rankings. Specific pages matter more than broad claims.

Industrial queries are narrow

A consumer brand can live on a handful of popular phrases. A component manufacturer usually cannot. The visits that matter name a product family, an operating limit, or an application. If the public site answers with a general claim about quality, both the engineer and the search engine have little to use. Industrial SEO is the discipline of matching those queries to pages that state what you sell and what you do not.

Technical audits sit underneath that. A large catalog can generate thousands of filter URLs, duplicate titles, and files that search tools cannot read. Until the indexable set is finite and internally linked, extra articles will not fix the problem. The audit is a list of what should be fetched and what should stay out of the index.

AI search uses the same facts, differently

Google, Bing, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity do not share one ranking method. Some retrieve live pages. Some answer from a mix of stored material and a search tool. Some show sources. Some do not. What you can control is whether the site states the product, the application, and the limit in sentences a specialist would accept. That helps a conventional results page. It also gives an assistant less reason to guess.

Answer-focused writing, citation checks, and entity work are easy to oversell. A clear page does not force a mention. An audit of how assistants describe the company is a qualitative check: ask a real buyer question, write down the date, and correct the public page when the answer invents a capability. Authority outside the site, such as a relevant directory or a technical publication, matters only when it matches what the site already says.

Language and location, without a second brochure

International and multilingual search is justified when you sell in that language and can maintain the specifications. A translated copy of a vague page does not open a country. Local pages are justified when a plant, a service area, or a stock location changes the offer. A city name pasted onto the homepage does not.

How search work depends on the site

An audit that is not followed by indexable product pages is a document. SEO copy that ignores the audit will be published on URLs you later ask search engines to drop. AI search preparation that is not tied to those same pages becomes a prompt exercise with nothing solid to summarize.

International pages and local pages should use the catalog you have already cleaned up. Entity and citation work belongs after the site describes the company accurately. Doing it first only spreads an unclear description into more places.
